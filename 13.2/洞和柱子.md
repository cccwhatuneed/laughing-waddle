一个普通人看拓扑学同伦的疑问

最近在看向量和空间拓扑的科普，看到一个关于“同伦”的概念，非常反直觉，心里一直觉得不对劲，想抛出来问问。

在数学定义里，如果空间里有一根皮筋（圈），被卡住缩不成点，那不管卡住它的是一个“实心柱子”，还是一个“空心坑”，只要皮筋都缩不回去，数学上就把它们当成同伦（同一类）。

但我顺着这个“小圈”反过来在现实里推演：
如果卡住这个圈的是一个柱子，或者是一个坑，这俩在现实物理里能是一回事吗？
一个能开过去，一个直接车毁人亡。物理性质完全相反啊。

如果以后搞 AI、搞机器人的工程师，为了算法简单，直接拿这种“把柱子和坑当成同一类”的数学逻辑去让 AI 认路，那 AI 是不是会把不可逾越的柱子，当成一个可以跨过去的小坑，然后直接撞死？

我不懂高深计算，只是觉得数学为了公式好看，把物理本质给抹平了。这事儿，现实里真的没隐患吗？

An Ordinary Person's Question About Topological Homotopy

Recently, I was reading some popular science on vectors and spatial topology, and I came across the concept of "homotopy." It's highly counter-intuitive, and it just doesn't feel right to me. I'd like to throw the question out there.

In the mathematical definition, if a rubber band (a loop) is trapped in space and can't be shrunk to a point, then whether it's trapped by a "solid pillar" or a "hollow pit," as long as the rubber band can't shrink back, math treats them as the same (homotopic).

But if I trace this "small loop" backward into reality: if what traps the loop is a pillar or a pit, are these two really the same in physical reality?
One can be driven over, while the other causes a fatal crash. Their physical properties are completely opposite!

If future engineers designing AI or robots, just for the sake of algorithmic simplicity, directly use this mathematical logic that treats "pillars and pits as the same" to let AI navigate, will the AI end up mistaking an impassable pillar for a passable pit, and just crash right into it?

I don't understand complex calculations. I just feel that math has flattened out physical reality just to make the formulas look good. In the real world, is there really no hidden danger here?
