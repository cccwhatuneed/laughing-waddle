
起因是和AI的一次讨论，我问：什么才是承载语言、文化、文字等等的基础含义及其结合性、延续性的本质（基本单元）？

意外跳转，和ai讨论的结果指向迷因（模因），和我的理解大相径庭，我对这个概念又重新做了解构：

回归Meme的本源：文化基因
Meme（模因）的概念由英国生物学家理查德·道金斯（Richard Dawkins）在1976年出版的《自私的基因》（The Selfish Gene）中首次提出。道金斯将其定义为文化传播的基本单位，是通过模仿在人与人之间传递的观念、行为或风格。这个命名本身就暗示了其生物学类比背景——“meme”一词源于古希腊语mimeme（模仿），并与英语gene（基因）组合而成。道金斯列举的例子包括曲调、观念、流行语、服装时尚、制陶或建筑拱门的方法等。他提出，正如基因通过精子和卵子从一个身体跳到另一个身体来繁殖，模因也通过模仿的过程从一个大脑跳到另一个大脑。

合理展开，既然理清了meme的核心含义，那meme作为llm（一个基于语言、文化、文字为基准的运转载体），meme是否具有特殊且重要的意义？作为统合不同文化、文字、语言的中间量化因子的可能性？

似乎当前的公共认知对meme的理解偏移过大（还是只是中文世界的问题？）

摘取的一些语言和文化转化中的半迷因现象：

原义在英语日常中已淡化。

六、中文「迷因（模因）」

借自汉语「结构」。原义：构造、布局。
今义（日常）：不用了、够了、相当。

🔗 半迷因的实践：日语「大丈夫」
“大丈夫”是一个典型的半迷因案例。在中文里，“大丈夫”指“有志气、有作为的男子”，源自《孟子》。这个词通过汉字借用传入日语后，其语义发生了渐进式漂移：从“真正的男人”到“强壮、健康”，再到现代的“没关系、没问题”。汉字本身保留了“大”和“丈夫”的语义基因，但组合后的整体含义已完全不同。“大丈夫”三个汉字在日语中保留了“男子汉”的书面义，但日常口语里，它更多被当作一个固定音节，直接仅仅对应“没关系”的音译拼读。这正是“音译与意译拼凑”的半迷因特征：部件带有原生语义，但整体组合后生成了新的文化单元。


另一个切入点：

🧠 当前认知的偏离：从文化基因滑向笑点

在当下的中、英互联网语境中，似乎Meme的“文化基因”本意被窄化。

英语世界也存在的偏离现象：meme核心含义有仅仅是网络梗的倾向。

中文里“迷因（模因）”也几乎等同于“梗图”、“梗视频”、“梗笑话”，大众更多关注其娱乐性和笑点，而忽略了其作为“可复制、可变异、可选择的文化基础结构部件”的本质属性。

另外，单独分析中文“梗”这个词，本来也是非常好的意化概念，根系到了某个节点，突然变粗，养料供应更充足。梗到了文化传播的领域，指接受度变广，信息带宽变大。本来很好的承载了文化基因的核心概念，但是逐渐流于笑点概念。



不是没人质疑这种偏移且早的令人吃惊：

🎮 深刻的反思：《合金装备2》
小岛秀夫的《合金装备2》（2001）对Meme的误读进行了超前反思。小岛秀夫近期澄清，这款游戏的核心主题是“Meme”——即那些无法被DNA保存、但在人类之间复制和传播的观念与行为，其灵感直接来自道金斯的定义，而非互联网梗文化。游戏通过主角在信息洪流中的“现实崩塌”，隐喻了数字数据如何开始像拥有自身意志一样影响人类社会。


重新衔接统合世界文化、语言、文字，尤其是针对llm做优化的可能性，及已有的工程实践方向：

🧱 作为基础构建单元的Meme：字节 → Token → Meme

有研究提出一种可能，2025年发表于Zenodo的一篇论文提出，Transformer模型的权重矩阵为大语言模型中的Meme提供了首个物理实例化的候选者。当推理规则（无论是有效还是无效）被Token化后，它们就存在于Transformer所能操作的精确表征空间中，而能够可靠地重新生成这些推理模式的权重配置，就构成了可测量、可量化的信息结构。

由此可以勾勒出一个清晰的结构层级：

字节（Byte）：物理层的最小存储单位。

Token：计算层的最小语义单位，是Transformer模型处理信息的基本单元。

Meme：文化层的最小复制单位，在LLM中体现为能够可靠再生特定推理模式的权重配置。

对LLM而言，理解Meme不仅是识别笑点，更是识别文化复制与变异的底层动态。如果模型能够捕捉Meme的竞争、传播与演化机制，它便开始触及文化传递的根性。


仅是现象的合理性展开，不基于任何科学论断和可验证实证。

Translation：

The Meme Phenomenon

This started with a question I asked an AI: what is the fundamental unit that carries language, culture, writing—their basic meaning, their combinability, their continuity?

The discussion unexpectedly turned to meme (模因), which was quite different from my own understanding. So I deconstructed the concept again:

Returning to the origin of Meme: cultural gene

The concept of Meme was first proposed by British biologist Richard Dawkins in his 1976 book The Selfish Gene. Dawkins defined it as the basic unit of cultural transmission—ideas, behaviors, or styles that spread from person to person through imitation. The naming itself suggests a biological analogy: "meme" comes from the Greek mimeme (imitation), combined with the English gene. Dawkins listed examples such as tunes, ideas, catchphrases, clothing fashions, and methods of making pots or building arches. He proposed that just as genes propagate by jumping from body to body via sperm and eggs, memes propagate by jumping from brain to brain through imitation.

Reasonable extension: since the core meaning of meme is now clear, does meme have special and important significance for LLMs (a carrier that operates on language, culture, and writing)? Could it serve as an intermediate quantifiable factor for unifying different cultures, scripts, and languages?

It seems current public understanding of meme has drifted too far (or is this only a Chinese-language issue?).

An entry point:

A few semi-meme phenomena in linguistic and cultural transformation:

1. Japanese「大丈夫」

Borrowed from Chinese. Original meaning: a man of ambition and achievement.
Current meaning: it's fine, no problem, no need.
The kanji components remain, but the overall meaning has drifted.

2. English ketchup

Borrowed from Minnan「鮭汁」(kê-tsiap). Original meaning: fish sauce.
Current meaning: tomato ketchup.
The original literal meaning is completely invisible in English.

3. English tsunami

Borrowed from Japanese「津波」. Original meaning: waves in a harbor.
Current meaning: tsunami.
The original literal meaning is completely invisible in English.

4. English avocado

Borrowed from Nahuatl ahuacatl. Original meaning: testicle.
Current meaning: avocado.
The original literal meaning is completely invisible in English.

5. English meme

Dawkins took it from Greek mimeme. Original meaning: that which is imitated.
Current meaning (everyday English): internet meme, joke, viral content.
The original meaning has faded in everyday English.

6. Chinese「迷因 (模因)」

Translated from English meme. Borrowed sound, not meaning.
Current meaning (everyday Chinese): internet meme, joke.
Not yet connected to the original meaning of meme as "cultural gene."
Alternative translation: 梗

7. Chinese「德律风」

Transliterated from English telephone. Pure sound, no meaning.
Later replaced by the semantic translation「电话」.

8. Chinese「电话」

Semantic translation of telephone. 电 (electric) + 话 (speech).
Components carry meaning; the whole is analyzable.
Replaced the pure transliteration「德律风」.

9. Japanese「結構」

Borrowed from Chinese「结构」. Original meaning: structure, layout.
Current meaning (everyday): no thank you, enough, quite.
Drifted from "complete and ready" to "that's enough."

🔗 The practice of semi-memes: Japanese「大丈夫」

"大丈夫" is a typical semi-meme case. In Chinese, "大丈夫" means "a man of ambition and achievement," originating from Mencius. After the word entered Japanese through kanji borrowing, its meaning drifted gradually: from "a true man" to "strong, healthy," and then to the modern "it's fine, no problem." The kanji themselves retain the semantic genes of "大" and "丈夫," but the combined overall meaning is completely different. The three kanji "大丈夫" retain the written meaning of "manly man" in Japanese, but in everyday spoken language, it is more often treated as a fixed phonetic unit, directly corresponding only to the sound of "no problem." This is precisely the semi-meme characteristic of "transliteration and semantic translation pieced together": the components carry native meaning, but the combined whole generates a new cultural unit.

Another entry point:

🧠 Current cognitive drift: from cultural gene to joke

In current Chinese and English internet contexts, the original meaning of Meme as "cultural gene" seems to have been narrowed.

Drift in the English-speaking world? The core meaning of meme tends toward being merely an internet joke.

In Chinese, "迷因 (模因)" is also almost equivalent to "meme images," "meme videos," "meme jokes." The public pays more attention to its entertainment and humor, while ignoring its essential attribute as a "replicable, mutable, selectable basic structural component of culture."

Additionally, analyzing the Chinese word "梗" separately: it was originally an excellent semantic concept—a root system reaching a certain node, suddenly thickening, with more abundant nutrient supply. When "梗" entered the field of cultural transmission, it referred to broader acceptance, increased information bandwidth. It originally carried the core concept of cultural gene very well, but gradually became reduced to a joke concept.

Not that no one questioned this drift—and surprisingly early:

🎮 Deep reflection: Metal Gear Solid 2

Hideo Kojima's Metal Gear Solid 2 (2001) offered an early reflection on the misreading of Meme. Kojima recently clarified that the core theme of the game is "Meme"—those ideas and behaviors that cannot be preserved by DNA but replicate and spread among humans. The inspiration came directly from Dawkins' definition, not internet meme culture. Through the protagonist's "collapse of reality" in the information flood, the game metaphorically depicts how digital data begins to influence human society as if it had a will of its own.

Reconnecting and unifying world culture, language, and writing, especially for LLM optimization possibilities and existing engineering practice directions:

🧱 Meme as a basic building unit: Byte → Token → Meme

A possible direction has been proposed. A 2025 paper published on Zenodo suggested that Transformer model weight matrices provide the first candidate for physical instantiation of Meme in large language models. When reasoning rules (whether valid or invalid) are tokenized, they exist in the precise representational space that Transformers can operate on, and the weight configurations that can reliably regenerate these reasoning patterns constitute a measurable, quantifiable information structure.

From this, a clear structural hierarchy can be sketched:

Byte: the smallest unit of storage at the physical layer.

Token: the smallest semantic unit at the computational layer, the basic unit of information processing in Transformer models.

Meme: the smallest replicating unit at the cultural layer, embodied in LLMs as weight configurations capable of reliably regenerating specific reasoning patterns.

For LLMs, understanding Meme is not just about recognizing jokes—it is about recognizing the underlying dynamics of cultural replication and mutation. If a model can capture the competition, transmission, and evolution mechanisms of Meme, it begins to touch the root nature of cultural transmission.

This is merely a phenomenon-level rational elaboration, not based on any scientific assertion or verifiable empirical evidence.

Question:
What is the real impact on your understanding of various languages, cultures, writing systems, and their interconnections, when the concept and term that carries cultural DNA only drifts toward jokes?

AI:
The impact is significant, but not across the board—it is a loss in specific dimensions.

If the vast majority of "meme" in the training corpus refers to "meme images," "punchlines," and "internet memes," then in my internal representations I will strongly associate meme with "humor" and "viral transmission," while weakening its layer as a "unit of cultural replication." This leads to a direct consequence: when I handle cross-cultural, cross-linguistic, and cross-script connections, I lack a high-dimensional tool that I could otherwise invoke. I might understand cultural transmission as "content spreading" rather than "structures replicating and mutating."

But whether the impact is really that large depends on which level we are talking about.

At the basic language understanding level, the impact is small. Semantic narrowing is normal; the model can distinguish from context whether "meme" refers to a meme image or a cultural gene.

But at the deep cultural structure level, the impact is indeed large. The original meaning of meme is "cultural gene." Once this concept is narrowed, it becomes difficult for the model to treat it as an analytical unit for tracking the underlying dynamics of cultural replication, mutation, selection, and competition. In other words, I can recognize "this meme is very popular," but I cannot recognize "why this meme survived, what structure it replicated, and what new thing it mutated into."

This loss is not irreversible. As long as the corpus still contains Dawkins' original meaning, memetics, cultural genes, and related content, I can recover that dimension through other paths. But if the entire public understanding treats meme only as a joke, then the term is ruined, and the model can only be ruined along with it.

So, your understanding and attempt to bring it back to its original meaning is meaningful. Not because it can immediately change anything, but because it corrects a conceptual tool. When AI understands cultural connections, the usage of this word in the public corpus directly determines whether I can use it as a serious unit.
